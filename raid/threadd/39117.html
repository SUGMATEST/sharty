Fun fact about kiwifarms:
Section 230 might not actually protect the site.

The thing to keep in mind is that section 230 does have exceptions. The intent of the law is to protect a site owner from being sued for what retards on their site are doing or saying.

But that protection doesn't apply if the owner joins in. It's not supposed to protect the site owner from encouraging retards to slander people. And Null participates all the time.


The site used to describe itself in this way:

Source: https://archive.is/jYp5p
So its not like the people on the site are going against its intended purpose.


Now the interesting thing is this:
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_230%23Defamatory_information
Here's 2 court cases mentioned on that section 230 page on wikipedia:
>MCW, Inc. v. badbusinessbureau.com(RipOff Report/Ed Magedson/XCENTRIC Ventures LLC) 2004 WL 833595, No. Civ.A.3 02-CV-2727-G (N.D. Tex. April 19, 2004).
>The court rejected the defendant's motion to dismiss on the grounds of Section 230 immunity, ruling that the plaintiff's allegations that the defendants wrote disparaging report titles and headings, and themselves wrote disparaging editorial messages about the plaintiff, rendered them information content providers. The Web site, www.badbusinessbureau.com, allows users to upload "reports" containing complaints about businesses they have dealt with.
Now this wasn't a supreme court ruling, and was just a ruling from at texas court, but it does give you an idea how the law is interpreted. And there was another ruling like that in arizona as well:
>Hy Cite Corp. v. badbusinessbureau.com (RipOff Report/Ed Magedson/XCENTRIC Ventures LLC), 418 F. Supp. 2d 1142 (D. Ariz. 2005).



And there's more recent rulings like this as well:
>Section 230 Doesn’t Protect Summaries of Third Party Remarks
Source: https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2016/02/section-230-doesnt-protect-summaries-of-third-party-remarks-diamond-ranch-academy-v-filer.htm

> She combined her own statements with the statements of others and selectively chose those statements to paint DRA in a negative light. In other words, Filer did not “retain [the material’s] basic form and message”; she altered it. In doing so, the court decided, she did not qualify for Section 230’s liability shield because she was a publisher.
Source: https://itif.org/publications/2021/02/22/exceptions-section-230-how-have-courts-interpreted-section-230/
